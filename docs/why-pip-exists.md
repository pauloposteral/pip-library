# Why PIP was born

PIP was born to bridge the gap between an idea and a verifiable result.

Artificial intelligence has made it easier than ever to imagine and prototype software. But a promising prototype is not necessarily a functioning product, a generated answer is not proof of completed work, and a passing demonstration is not sufficient evidence of release readiness.

PIP exists to make these differences visible. Creators should be able to understand what was planned, what was actually built, what was tested, what remains uncertain, and what is ready to ship.

The public library begins with practical skills, templates and evidence conventions that people can inspect and adapt. Future hosted products must earn trust through observed behavior and independent verification. Their existence on a roadmap is not evidence that they work today.
